What actually changes by wrapper

Costa Rica taxes source, not citizenship. A hostel in San José or Tamarindo produces Costa Rican-source income regardless of whether the owner lives in Easton, PA or Escazú. The United States taxes its citizens on worldwide income and has no income-tax treaty with Costa Rica. That combination — not the hostel’s bunk count — drives most of the entity decision.

Platforms are being pulled into withholding (Airbnb 12.75% of payout flagged for late-2026; Booking/Expedia already in the digital-services net for CR cards).

Incentives that are real

  • Law 6990 (Incentivos para el Desarrollo Turístico) can waive import duties on FF&E and grant other relief after an ICT Declaratoria Turística / contrato turístico. That is a hotel-grade filing, not a weekend patent.
  • Law 9996 investor / retiree package: $150k qualifying investment can support inversionista residency; household-goods / vehicle import relief has been time-limited (verify current decree — the 2026 window has been discussed through mid-year).
  • MEIC micro/small CIT holidays exist for registered PYMEs (0% years 1–3, then partial). A capitalized tourist lodging business often fails the spirit or the size test.
  • Hiring locals is not optionally “incentivized”; it is mandatory if you want legal labor. Caja inscription is the price of a work force.

Incentives that get oversold

  • There is no automatic income-tax holiday for “bringing tourism.” Free-zone / Ley 7210 is for exporters and qualified service companies, not a Barrio California bunkhouse.
  • Faith-based or nonprofit clothing does not zero-out IVA or let you skip Caja.
  • US FEIE is not a 100% shield on hostel profit. Capital in the building caps the earned slice; distributions from a sociedad are dividends.
  • Foreigners already may title ordinary (non-maritime-zone) real estate in their own name. A company is optional for ownership, useful for liability and banking.

How the country actually segments

Hotel chamber peak-season surveys (CCH, Dec 2025 for YE25/YE26) are not annual hostel occupancy. They do show the map: Northern Plains 92% projected peak, Guanacaste 88%, Central Valley city 60–68%, South Pacific ~70%. Airbnb-style inventory roughly doubled since 2019 and prints national occupancy in the low-to-mid 30s to mid-40s depending on the dataset — that is the competitive floor under private rooms. Hostel dorms still clear better than lonely Airbnbs because of social demand, but they are not immune to oversupply in Tamarindo / Jaco / Puerto Viejo.

2026 labor load — the part Gemini flattened

Employer social charges rose on 1 Jan 2026 because IVM went from 5.42% to 5.58%. Headline employer package is 26.83% if you include INA (1.50%, skipped under 5 workers), FODESAF 5%, IMAS 0.50%, Banco Popular slices, FCL 1.50%, complementary pension 2.00%, and ~1% INS riesgos del trabajo (activity-rated). That 26.83% is not the fully loaded cost. Aguinaldo is a 13th month (8.33% of base, paid 1–20 December). Vacaciones are two weeks after 50 weeks. Many operators also reserve cesantía. Fact audit of the prior model & the Gemini memo

Directionally Gemini was right about one thing: at $13 bunks, labor + occupancy cost can eat the room. The supporting numbers were rounded folklore. This desk replaces them with 2026 public sources and says where the tape is thin.

A well-run SJ transit hostel can do mid-50s to mid-60s annual; a beach poshtel can beat that in dry months and fall into the 30s in October. Seasonality is the model, not a single σ.

Corporate tax “5–30%” Quoted as if every hostel picks a rate from a menu. 30% is the ordinary CIT. 5/10/15/20% is an SME scale gated by annual gross. Dividend WHT to a non-resident owner is 15% (5% listed-share exception). Law 9428 annual company tax is extra.
US FEIE on “the hostel” Implied the profit lands in the exclusion. FEIE 2026 cap $132,900 is for foreign earned income. Dividends from an S.R.L. are not earned. If capital is material, IRS caps the earned slice at 30% of net. No US–CR income tax treaty. FTC is the tool for CIT/WHT. FBAR / 8938 / 5471 still exist.
Nonprofit = 0% Table cell said 0% if “public utility.” Utilidad pública is a political/administrative grant, not a checkbox. IVA still hits commercial guest charges. Hacienda audits mixed-purpose associations. Do not underwrite a yield hostel as a church.
IVA Missing from both the HTML app and the Gemini P&L. 13% on short-stay lodging, F&B, most extras. Collect and remit. Platforms are being deputized as withhold agents.
OTA 15% of total revenue Applied to F&B too. Commission is on the booked room (and sometimes packages). Bar tabs and laundry at the desk are not Hostelworld’s. Use an OTA mix on room revenue only.
Insurance $200–900 / month as “utils” Lumped into a wide band with water. INS hogar-style quotes on a $600k house can be ~$1.6k/year. Commercial RC + fire on a hostel is higher than a house but Gemini’s high monthly print looks like replacement-cost coastal luxury, not a SJ fan house.
$72k rent as destiny Hard-coded in both scenarios. Hotel listings median asking ~$1.35m nationally; commercial median asking ~$799k — means are higher. SJ apartments ~$2,000–3,000/m². Tamarindo median asking on tracked inventory is luxury-tilted (~$690k). Foreigners rarely get cheap long amortizing CRC mortgages. Underwrite lease vs purchase as two models.
FX Silent. September 2026 colón around ₡447, strongest since 2007 records. You collect dollars and pay wages in colones — until you don’t. A strong colón compresses USD margins even when arrivals are up (ICT: 2.19m arrivals Jan–Aug 2026, +5.3% vs 2025, with a soft August US print).
Law 6990 as a free lunch Mentioned as property-tax holidays and duty-free FF&E. Real statute. Real ICT process. Not automatic for a 48-bed hostel that looks like a converted house. Model zero incentive until a lawyer and an architect have a filing plan.
